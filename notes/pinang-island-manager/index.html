Nine months as an island GM. What Pinang taught me

It started as curiosity and a dream of living the surfer’s life in Sumatra. It turned into a school of management with no weekends or rest time.

Milo Brzák · No playbook notes · November 2026

I was the general manager of Pinang, an island off Sumatra. After the agreed period, it was time to say bye to paradise. What started as curiosity and a dream of living the surfer’s life turned into a journey full of unique skills and lessons. With no weekends or rest time.

Nine months of crisis management, logistics and safety at sea. Twice I had to decide whether to send a boat out into a storm to take someone to a hospital more than 30 hours away. Both evacuations ended well.

What I picked up along the way

  • Prioritise the guest experience. Tasks have to be managed effectively, but the guest always comes first.
  • Safety first. When a storm hits, making sure everyone and everything is safe is the top priority, no matter what else is on the to-do list.
  • Roll with the punches. Every day brings surprises, especially when things go off-plan. Finding solutions and learning from those moments is key.
  • Embrace diversity. Working with people from different backgrounds taught me to balance getting things done with having fun, to keep the team spirit high.
  • Language. Communication is critical, especially when almost no one on the team speaks English.
  • Be there for your team. Step in and help when needed, and respect how hard your team works.
  • Be honest and transparent. Always be upfront with the board about what’s going well and what needs improvement.

Things to keep in mind

Sometimes solutions come faster when you’re not rushing. Make time for yourself so you can be more effective later. Be thoughtful about your to-do list and set priorities. And be a steady presence for the team, no matter what’s going on.

Drinking less coffee might be better. But damn, Sumatra coffee is so tasty. And when coconuts are everywhere, why not have one a day.

Have a thought on this? I’d be glad to hear a different view.
