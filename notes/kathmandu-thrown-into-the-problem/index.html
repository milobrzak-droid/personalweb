What changed me · Kathmandu

Thrown straight into the problem. What an American team at Kathmandu taught me

After years of running my own businesses, I started again inside a global company on the other side of the world.

Milo Brzák · No playbook notes · November 2026

I joined Kathmandu in New Zealand as Quality Coordinator. No long onboarding. Right away I was given one of the company’s most complex problems: overseas supply of a hero product. A product that drove a significant part of the company’s economics across the portfolio.

I didn’t know retail from this side. I had to learn fast: the customer relationship, product quality and the whole chain from the factory to the store. I led the analysis, prepared the material, reported to the company’s leadership and coordinated people across teams.

How a team that’s used to scaling works

Kathmandu is a global company, and I worked in an American team with top people. A few things I still carry with me:

  • Data before opinion. A discussion started with numbers, not impressions. If you wanted to change something, you had to show why.
  • Every problem has one owner. Not a team, not a department. A specific person who knows it’s theirs.
  • If it isn’t written down, it doesn’t exist. Status, decisions and next steps were written so anyone could pick them up, in any time zone.
  • A short rhythm. Regular reporting to leadership wasn’t control. It was a way to keep the problem moving.

What I do with it today

This is a language you don’t need in a small company. Until you start growing. Then you need it immediately, and nobody teaches it to you.

At Enter, I now build the same principles into systems. A clear owner, data on the table, decisions written down. The only difference is that agents now handle part of that work, and people keep the judgment.

Have a thought on this? I’d be glad to hear a different view.
