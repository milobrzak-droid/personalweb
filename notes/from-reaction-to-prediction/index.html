A company can’t grow faster than its people can decide

Why I think scaling starts with prediction, not with more people putting out fires.

Milo Brzák · No playbook notes · November 2026

Most companies don’t grow as fast as they could. Often not because of the market, but because of how decisions get made.

A decision comes only once something has happened. A customer complains, material is missing, an invoice is overdue, someone leaves. Leadership deals with what has already happened. Up to a certain size, that works. Then it breaks.

With every new customer, order and person, there are more events to react to. When they all end up with a few people, the company grows only as fast as those people can keep up. The founder becomes the bottleneck. Not because they don’t want to delegate. They’re just the only one with the full picture.

Scaling doesn’t mean adding people to the reaction

It means moving decisions earlier.

A company has most of the information it needs long before a problem shows. It’s in the orders, the emails, the numbers and people’s experience. It just isn’t connected, and nobody looks at it in time.

That’s why at Enter we build systems that connect that data and watch it continuously. An agent notices a delivery is slipping before the customer does. It prepares the material. A person then decides earlier and with better information. We call it from reaction to prediction.

Prediction isn’t fortune-telling

It’s a discipline. Knowing which signals to watch, who owns which decision and what should happen when a signal comes. Technology makes it possible. But the company has to get clear on it first.

On an island off Sumatra, I twice had to decide whether to send a boat out into a storm to get someone to a hospital. That’s where I learned what a decision looks like when it comes late and with incomplete information. In a company, it doesn’t have to be that way.

The question I keep asking: which decision in our company could we make a week earlier if we looked at the right data?

Have a thought on this? I’d be glad to hear a different view.
